# Search priorities

Copy this to `jobs/priorities.md` and rewrite it as your own. That file is gitignored —
these are your constraints, not the repo's.

Phase B of the `/jobhunt` loop reads it before triaging anything, so vague entries here
produce vague recommendations. Write the hard filters as hard filters.

`jobs/settings.json` holds what a rule can check — where you may work, role families,
seniority bands, relocation targets. This file is for the rest: the judgement a title alone
cannot support. Keep the two in step; see [README.md](README.md#making-it-yours).

---

## 1. The non-negotiable

The one thing that makes an otherwise perfect posting an automatic no. Most searches have
exactly one. Examples: must be outside your current country; must be fully remote; must not
require relocation; must clear a salary floor.

> Replace this with yours, stated as a filter rather than a wish.

## 2. Location, in tiers

Rank the arrangements you would accept, best first, so a lead can be compared against
another rather than judged alone.

1. …
2. …
3. …

Name your relocation targets explicitly — a country you would move to is worth applying to
even when the role is off-theme.

## 3. Preferred role themes

The domains you actively want, in order. These earn a bonus, they are not requirements.

## 4. Role families, and the seniority band for each

**Judge seniority per family, never globally.** The years that make you a credible Senior in
the work you were paid for do not transfer to the family you have only done as a hobby, and
a blanket "no senior roles" rule throws away leads you could win.

For each family you would apply to, write down the ceiling and the reason:

| Family | Band | Why |
|---|---|---|
| e.g. QA | up to Senior | five years of paid experience |
| e.g. Game design | entry / internship only | personal projects, no paid work |

## 5. Least wanted

The family you would take but would rather not. Ranking it last is not the same as
excluding it — say which.

---

## Eligibility is a blocker, not a preference

Keep this separate from location *preference*. "Remote (US)" nearly always means
*authorized to work in the US*; if you are not, that is a wall, not an inconvenience.

List the phrasings that disqualify you outright:

- work authorization you do not hold, or "no visa sponsorship"
- an employer-of-record limited to countries you are not in
- a timezone floor you cannot cover from where you live
- degree gates, security clearances, named stacks you have never used

These are the constraints the scorer cannot see, because they live in the body of the job
description rather than the title. Finding them is most of what phase B is for.
